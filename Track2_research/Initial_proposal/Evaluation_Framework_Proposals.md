# Evaluation framework proposals

## Recommendation

Choose A, a matched evidence-flow audit with uncertainty and independent span validation. The Track 2 baseline comes from Liu et al.’s independent trajectory diagnosis on BrowseComp-Plus. Jingfen’s PDF is Track 1 context. Use a limited B analysis if the archived query joins are good. Add C as the budgeted navigation view of A if event ordering and output observability support it. Keep separate baselines for outcomes and resource use. An aggregate score would conceal retrieval and exposure differences.

## Definitions shared across proposals

For question i, let G be the benchmark gold documents, H the separate evidence qrels, W(t) the staged set and O(t) the body spans verifiably delivered to the model by budget t. An exposure label has levels verified yes, documented negative where observation is adequate, and unresolved. The older audit label “no documented event” is weaker than verified absence. A manually defined clue can have multiple acceptable spans. A sufficient set is an adjudicated combination of clues that resolves the question given the actual text. Keep the benchmark answer and answer judge separate from the evidence labels. In parallel search, measure a query batch jointly or report member gains against the shared prior state and their overlap.

## A. Where is the extra evidence lost?

**RQ1.** On matched BM25 and SPLADE questions, how often do workspace coverage gains survive to verified exposure and sufficient observed evidence? Hypothesis: workspace improvements are larger than exposure improvements. This addresses Jingfen's distinction between construction and utilization and adapts AtlasNav and HotpotQA. Use paired final recall, any/all-gold coverage, attributable body access, any-source decisive exposure, annotated clue coverage and sufficiency, then matched verdicts. For every rate declare the denominator. Report conditional P(verified body access | gold path documented) only for the same source when matching events. Across arms report the full pair table, the shared opportunity stratum where both workspaces contain the same gold source, and the extra-gold stratum separately. These strata are descriptive and can select different questions.

**RQ2.** When the agent answers incorrectly after receiving sufficient evidence, is the remaining failure an incomplete answer, a source conflict, an unsupported inference, or a judge disagreement? Hypothesis: confirmed sufficiency-before-error cases exist but are a subset of all errors. Report the joint rate of sufficient exposure and incorrect answer, plus conditional accuracy with denominators and case coding. Use RAGChecker for claim-level inspiration and ALCE for the distinction between answer and support. Independent review of q229 is a useful test of the codebook. q486 is a contrast involving source conflict. Do not assume either case generalizes.

**Implementation and contribution.** One to two person-days for extraction and paired figures, then two to four person-days of shared review for a small validation set, depending on annotation time. No GPU or Slurm for A. Outcomes: a paired flow table, heatmap of stage discordance, uncertainty panel, and annotated trajectories including a case where SPLADE succeeds. Baselines are the two same-judge runs, the conventional coverage/accuracy table and the document-level diagnostic of [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01913). For each incorrect run, label the inventory analogue of their utilization gap as any labeled gold staged and the retrieval gap as no gold staged. Also show their test with surfaced gold as the nearest analogue to their returned results, clearly specifying the stage. Among incorrectly answered, gold-staged cases, report counts and fractions by verified insufficient exposure, verified sufficient exposure and unresolved. Publish a confusion/cross-tabulation against the baseline and two cases where the diagnosis changes. Have reviewers score sufficiency without seeing the baseline label. These counts test incremental diagnostic resolution rather than a causal claim. The added contribution is auditable diagnosis under imperfect logging. Threats include correlated stages, incomplete logs, adjudication influenced by expected answers, dataset-specific clues, and uncontrolled serving differences.

## B. Do reformulations add new evidence at matched query budgets?

**RQ3.** Under a fixed ranker, how quickly do logged agent queries add distinct relevant documents relative to full-question-only retrieval and the first agent query? Hypothesis: some later queries contribute new coverage but gains diminish. Define rank-list R(s,K) for query s. Marginal gold gain is |G intersect (R(s,K) minus W_before)| / |G|. Report independent relevance, cumulative union recall over the first j subqueries and first b search batches, and normalized exact repeats. Report both coverage versus number of slots and coverage versus unique workspace size. For batches compute union gain and overlap rather than attributing a gain by arbitrary serialized order.

Pair questions, cross-rank both arms' exact logged strings with both frozen rankers at matched K where archived, and include full question, first query and logged sequence. Preserve occurrence order after joining the deduplicated manifest. Report the fraction of trajectories and slots eligible. TREC CAsT and ECAsT motivate fixed-pipeline reformulation comparisons, and TREC Dynamic Domain supplies novelty and budget motivation. Ning et al.’s CTAR may be used on observable outputs as a secondary lexical diagnostic. SearchAtlas, a Track 1 cross-reference, supports explicit query-to-evidence links. Stronger evidence-conditioned quality would require labeled prior clues or controlled continuation with and without those clues, as in Bridge Evidence. Query choices depend on earlier results. Frozen re-ranking identifies retrieval behavior for observed strings. Comparing which arm generated better strings is descriptive under this design. Estimate two to three person-days if matches are sufficient. Show ranker-by-query-source curves and query examples. Defer new rankings if the archives are sparse or incompatible.

## C. How efficiently does navigation turn available leads into evidence?

**RQ4.** By fixed decision budgets, how often is a shared reachable evidence target actually exposed, and which recorded failures explain delays? Hypothesis: path sightings and model-visible span exposure diverge, especially with clipping or repeated range errors. Select the same gold target in both workspaces before examining outcomes. Lead conversion is the fraction of unique eligible visible `(question, document)` leads that receive a successful body access within h subsequent model decisions. Report h=3 and h=5, leads near termination as a separate group, and a companion all-lead measure treating terminal trajectories as having no further observed reads. Use the first subsequent model decision after the lead became visible. Validate mappings for grep paths, native reads and direct decisive shell output. Plot verified exposure by 5, 10 and 20 model decisions with all questions retained, opportunity bounds using observable output, plus redundant line overlap and explicit error/clipping rates.

For Track 2, Liu et al. motivate testing the relationship between effort and evidence. AtlasNav and Is Grep All You Need? are Track 1 cross-references for fixed-budget realization and delivery controls, respectively. Existing archives give a retrospective comparison. An optional test holding an identical workspace, layout, model and budget while varying only preview/read delivery would be the stronger harness baseline. Applicability to DCI or inline retrieval needs a shared evidence target and decision/token budgets, rather than matching native tool names. Estimate one to two person-days for a limited trace panel plus manual audit. Report exposed evidence versus budget and annotated follow-up failures. Missing timing is a risk for exact within-batch conversions.

## Ranking

| Rank | Proposal | Value and alignment | Data and effort | Strength of attainable conclusions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Directly addresses the whole-pipeline question and adds validation beyond the existing stage counts | High availability, moderate human effort | Strong descriptive localization with explicit uncertainty |
| 2 | C | Directly tests workspace utilization and evidence delivery | Good for selected events, limited by prefixes and clipping | Strong for verified cases, weaker for undocumented failures |
| 3 | B | Answers the query formulation question and separates ranking from policy on fixed strings | Good archived starting point, exact coverage unresolved | Strong for covered fixed strings, weak for the causal value of reformulation |

## Practical implementation

### Phase 1 — Existing data

Freeze and document the run pair, code/corpus identifiers, qrels, limits and recorded provider configuration. Reuse `search_analysis.py` path mapping, tool-family and trace loaders, `breadth_depth.py` for step classification and its exact McNemar implementation, and `retrieval_recall.py` for qrel and ranked-list readers. New scripts proposed for later implementation: `evidence_events.py` to export a question/run/turn/call event table with separate full/prefix/reconstructed/unknown provenance, `evidence_eval.py` for paired metrics and bounds, and `query_rank_crosswalk.py` for the archive join. Keep frozen annotations and derived CSVs separate from the raw experiment files. Emit final-level stages and matched-source transitions, pair counts, the document-gap versus span-diagnosis table, and verified-exposure curves. Do not call an ordinary survival estimate an exposure probability when early stopping is informative. Report “ever verified by budget,” retain stopped runs, and explicitly bound unknowns.

### Phase 2 — Validation

Develop the codebook on 6–8 varied pilot pairs. Include q288 as a navigation case, q1038 as a cap/provenance case, and q229 as a sufficiency/answer case. Use a separate balanced set of roughly 20–24 pairs covering both-correct, both-wrong and both discordant directions. Report the exact sampling scheme. Mask retriever and answer verdict when scoring observed evidence, then adjudicate. Compare extractor and human labels for eligibility, source, span and sufficiency. Report raw agreement and confusion counts with class-wise precision/recall on adjudicated visible events, plus a chance-corrected measure where meaningful. Independently test parser fixtures for same-batch calls, direct grep evidence, clipping and prefix-only reads. Keep alternate source evidence and multi-clue requirements eligible. Report sampled validation separately from full-set estimates or use sampling weights. Estimate uncertainty by bootstrapping question pairs and using exact McNemar for fully observed binary outcomes or verified indicators with that estimand labeled. Report missingness bounds separately and treat secondary comparisons as exploratory.

### Phase 3 — Optional experiments

Only after the audit, preregister one targeted comparison. For a delivery question, hold candidate set, model, layout, prompt, seeds and budget fixed and vary the rendering. For a causal bridge question, replay a safe matched prefix under full, target-span removed and length-matched distractor conditions, then compare progress and answers over repeated continuations. An evidence-supplied condition measures performance with access granted. Server calls, GPU or Slurm require separate authorization if needed. Current evaluation uses saved evidence only. Stronger generalization would require comparable records from a second interface or a held-out split.

**Minimum viable result.** Publish paired stage counts with provenance and unknowns, one exposure-at-budget plot, two contrasting cases, and inter-reviewer agreement from the validation subset. The strongest follow-up is evidence sufficiency and cross-condition validation, with query replay optional if it has adequate coverage.
