# Evaluation metrics literature review

## What the meeting suggests

Miguel's notes suggest evaluating three distinct decisions: how an agent forms queries, what enters its workspace, and what it actually sees as it navigates. This is an interpretation of the meeting reconstruction. Jingfen's five-layer literature map adds two distinctions that matter for measurement. Execution can change latency while preserving returned text, and corpus organization can change what fits in the text the model sees. For instance, GrepSeek's pruning can preserve matches while RARG changes their order under a cap. Measure output equivalence, exposed evidence and execution cost independently.

## Closest work

| Primary work | What the authors evaluate | Annotation and applicability to IR2 | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [RISE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06880) and [official implementation](https://github.com/texttron/RISE) | Answer accuracy, cost and runtime across workspace and baseline variants, plus retrieval depth and corpus scaling. Top-K per query builds an explorable union while previews expose a smaller set. | Existing final verdicts, qrels and inventories reproduce end points. Plotting a passage-level trajectory is our adaptation. | A final inventory has no per-document arrival times. RISE interfaces bundle workspace, preview and navigation differences. |
| [BrowseComp-Plus](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1023/) | Fixed corpus, benchmark answer judging, gold and evidence retrieval, comparison across retrievers and agents. | Gold/evidence qrels join to inventories and rankings. They label documents. | Labeled evidence is incomplete and need not exhaust alternate routes or identify the sufficient span. |
| [AtlasNav: Evidence Blindness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24764) | Stage-wise realization from evidence surfacing through opening and fragment localization, budgeted realization, and same-model evidence-supplied reference. | Closest precedent for our stage and budget curves. Local adaptation needs attributable text and criteria for a complete evidence set. | Its navigation policy and observation schema differ. Treat evidence-supplied performance as an empirical reference under a changed information condition. |
| [SearchAtlas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10901) | Builds evidential query graphs linking earlier observations, queries, question constraints and answers. Checks extracted edges against human labels and reports mean edge F1 of 86%. | Adapt a small subset of edge types with paired human review. Full graph reconstruction is risky with prefix-only records. | An inferred provenance edge is observational. Reliable labels for query dependence are expensive. |
| [Bridge Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253) | Intervenes on previously observed evidence and measures changes to subsequent queries, answers and search cost. Separates static relevance from counterfactual usefulness. | Use its intervention design for a future continuation pilot. Current logs can label a proposed bridge and record later reuse. | Removing text can change coherence, budget and subsequent state. Saved observational traces alone cannot reproduce its causal estimand. |
| [Is Grep All You Need?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184) | Compares grep and vector tools across harnesses and studies inline versus file-based delivery with answer performance and efficiency. | Borrow factorial controls and track observation budget. Saved RISE traces permit descriptive comparisons only. | Its task, tools and harnesses differ. Tool effects can depend on the delivery interface. |
| [Filesystem-Based Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26637) | Varies memory organization, tool interfaces and scale, tracking answer quality, cost and store health. | Reuse the quality–cost reporting principle. Filesystem evolution and maintenance are outside the saved RISE runs. | A continually updated memory store is a different setting from a fixed corpus and a transient question workspace. |

## Complementary work

| Primary work | Method and actual evaluation | Transfer and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| [TREC Session Track](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec19/papers/SESSION.OVERVIEW.2010.pdf) and [Dynamic Domain](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec26/papers/Overview-DD.pdf) | Session DCG discounts across rank and queries. Dynamic Domain uses subtopic judgments and gain sensitive to novelty and cost. | Qrels and archived rankings permit rank and marginal coverage analyses. Use plain gain curves as primary. A human examination discount does not encode RISE's top-10 preview plus grep interface. Subtopic coverage needs claim labels. |
| [How AI agents search](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0360157) | Controlled search logs support analysis of repeated queries, reformulation transitions, decomposition and search effectiveness. | Exact normalized repeats and query transitions can be measured. Label semantic changes or clue incorporation on a sample. Repetition can be legitimate when state or retrieval scope changes. |
| [HotpotQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09600) | Sentence-level supporting facts and joint QA/support evaluation on multi-hop questions. | Borrow span annotation granularity and explicit joint success. BrowseComp-Plus qrels cannot substitute for supporting facts, and a single reference span can omit valid alternatives. |
| [RAGChecker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08067) | Claim-level entailment diagnostics include retriever claim recall and generator-side context utilization and hallucination measures. | Adapt claim coverage on observed evidence with human review. Needs defensible claim decomposition and entailment. Flattening the entire workspace into context would falsely credit unseen evidence. |
| [ALCE](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/) | Answer correctness and citation quality, including entailment-based citation recall and precision. | Useful if a future harness emits span attributions. Short uncited answers permit manual answer support, with citation precision undefined and causal use unobserved. |
| [Cross-Lingual BrowseComp-Plus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15345) | Answer accuracy, evidence recall, search behavior, citation fidelity and oracle retrieval under changed evidence language. | Illustrates usefulness of evidence-supplied comparisons even when retrieval is strong. Requires new continuations and does not isolate the BM25/SPLADE mechanism. |
| [GrepSeek](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29307), [Dr-DCI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14885), [RARG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24223), [IndexAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07960) | These systems vary shell training and efficient execution, dynamic pulling, relevance-ordered scanning, or persistent index-state operations respectively. Jingfen's map helps specify their differing observation boundaries. | Use them to motivate two controls: change search execution holding its output fixed, or change exposure holding the candidate set fixed. Per-tool counts across these systems are incomparable without a common model-decision or token axis. Their logs are outside the paired audit. |

## Contribution and scope

The broad idea of tracing evidence through stages is already established, especially in AtlasNav and SearchAtlas. IR2 can contribute an auditable evaluation of whether changing a retriever yields useful progress under a common RISE interface. The proposed additions are matched question-pair accounting, separation of observed failures from incomplete recording, a span-based audit with masked second ratings, and a cross-ranking probe on frozen queries. This is a claim about the design and findings of the IR2 study. A claim that the method transfers would require a second comparable trajectory condition. Use a compact metric panel instead of a weighted score, which could hide the very tradeoff the project seeks to explain.

Project sources include `context/Grep Search Literature.pdf`, `context/Towards Retrieving Interaction Spaces for Agentic Search.pdf`, `context/IR2 RISE reproduction runs.xlsx`, `meetings/IR2_meeting5.md`, and the saved audits described in the feasibility report.
